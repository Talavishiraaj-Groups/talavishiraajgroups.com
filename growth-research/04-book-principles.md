## 1. Sheridan, *They Ask You Answer* — the single most applicable book in the library

The core discovery, verbatim in substance: buyers obsess over five subjects, and
businesses "generally ignore or even hide from these questions." [FACT — p.1838ff]

**The Big 5:**
1. **Pricing and cost**
2. **Problems** (your own weaknesses, and the problems with your category)
3. **Versus and comparisons**
4. **Reviews**
5. **Best in class** (who the best providers are, including competitors)

Also: *"The Critical Need for Unbiased Content"* and *"Disarmament: The Quickest
Way to Build Trust."* [FACT — ch.18]

> **Applied to Talavishiraaj:** all five are absent from the site. Price is
> hidden. No page names a problem, a limitation, or a case where Prism is the
> wrong purchase. No comparison of any kind. No review, testimonial or named
> client. No "who else is good at this" content. The Big 5 is simultaneously the
> content backlog and the trust strategy, and it is 0% built.
>
> This is not a theoretical match. The competitor verified to rank for these
> queries (Covision — see `03`) executes the Big 5 almost line by line: an open
> cost table, a warning-signs list, an audit-vs-audit comparison table, and its
> own proprietary failure statistic.

## 2. Hormozi, *$100M Offers* — the offer is the lever, not the traffic

**The Value Equation** [FACT — ch.6]:

```
        Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
         Perceived Time Delay  ×  Perceived Effort & Sacrifice
```

Hormozi's own correction of his early career is the important part: *"larger-than-
life claims are the easiest to establish (and therefore less unique). After all,
anyone can make a promise. The harder, and more competitive, are the Time Delay
and Effort & Sacrifice. The best companies focus all their attention on the bottom
side of the equation."* [FACT]

And: *"perception is reality"* — the London Underground dot-matrix board raised
rider satisfaction more than faster trains would have, because it attacked
*perceived* wait. [FACT]

Other operative lines:
- *"The pain is the pitch."* [FACT]
- *"The point of good writing is for the reader to understand. The point of good
  persuasion is for the prospect to feel understood."* [FACT]
- Purchasing power is a qualifier: his friend's résumé service failed because the
  buyers were all unemployed. Pick a market that can pay. [FACT]

> **Applied to Talavishiraaj:** Prism already wins the bottom of the equation and
> does not say so. 10–13 business days against an observed market norm of 2–6
> weeks (see `03`) is a genuine Time Delay advantage. Fixed scope and fixed fee is
> a genuine Effort & Sacrifice advantage. The site states the timeline as a neutral
> logistical fact inside a policy paragraph, rather than as the reason to buy.
>
> Meanwhile the top of the equation is empty: no dream outcome stated in the
> buyer's terms and, with zero named clients or figures, no mechanism at all for
> Perceived Likelihood of Achievement. The site attacks neither half.

## 3. Miller, *Building a StoryBrand* — the clarity test the site fails

**The grunt test:** *"Could a caveman look at your website and immediately grunt
what you offer?"* — resolving three questions: what do you offer, how will it make
my life better, what do I do to buy it. [FACT — p.711]

*"The days of using our website as a clearinghouse of information are over...
Today your website should be the equivalent of an elevator pitch."* [FACT — p.3546]

And the structural insight of SB7: **the customer is the hero, the company is the
guide.** Companies that cast themselves as the hero lose.

> **Applied to Talavishiraaj:** "Solving complex problems with research and
> engineering" fails the grunt test outright — it names no deliverable, no buyer
> and no outcome. The home page is a clearinghouse: 6 capabilities × 6 buyer types
> × 5 differentiators. And the copy is relentlessly hero-positioned — "we bring
> together", "we execute", "we are not a generic agency", "we are vendor neutral".
> Miller's diagnosis is exact: the company is the hero of its own site.

## 4. Miller, *How to Grow Your Small Business* — relevant to the buyer, not just the seller

The book's premise (a business as an aeroplane: leadership, marketing, sales,
products, overhead/operations) is *itself* the problem Prism diagnoses.

> [INFER] This is a content asset, not just a lesson. The company sells to
> founder-led businesses that have outgrown informal working. The literature those
> buyers already read — Miller, EOS/*Traction*, *The E-Myth* — is a ready-made
> vocabulary for meeting them where they already are, instead of inventing
> "workflow intelligence".

## 5. Blanks & Jesson, *Making Websites Win* — the constraint that rules out testing

*"The biggest mistake people make when optimizing their websites is what we call
'meek tweaking.' They set up A/B testing software, then they make daft changes.
They change button colors and shuffle items around the page, just because they
read that it worked for someone else."* And: in A/B tests, small improvements take
disproportionately longer to detect. [FACT — p.1622ff]

> **Applied to Talavishiraaj:** decisive, and constraining. At this traffic level
> A/B testing is statistically impossible — there will never be enough sessions to
> detect anything. Therefore: **no A/B testing, no conversion-rate targets, no
> split tests in the plan.** Conversion work must be large principled rewrites
> validated qualitatively (session recordings, five-second tests, sales-call
> transcripts, asking actual buyers), not quantitatively.
>
> [INFER] Any plan that proposes CRO-by-testing here is wrong on the arithmetic.

## 6. Krug, *Don't Make Me Think* / Yablonski, *Laws of UX* / Weinschenk, *Neuro Web Design*

The usable core for this case:
- Users scan, satisfice and click the first plausible option; they do not read.
- Jakob's Law: people expect your site to work like the sites they already know.
  Inventing novel category vocabulary imposes a learning cost the visitor will not
  pay.
- Social proof, authority and scarcity operate pre-consciously; their *absence* is
  itself read as a signal.

> **Applied:** the six-bucket capability architecture forces a choice on a visitor
> who does not yet know what they need. A visitor who cannot self-identify in five
> seconds leaves. The absence of any human face or name is not neutral — it reads
> as an absence of accountability, on a site whose central claim is accountability.

## 7. Ash, *Landing Page Optimization* / Smith, *The Conversion Code*

Ash: match the page to the visitor's **stage of awareness**; one page cannot serve
a problem-unaware and a solution-ready visitor. Smith: speed of response to an
inbound lead dominates every other sales variable.

> **Applied:** the site offers exactly one conversion action — "Talk to our team" /
> "Initiate Engagement", a high-commitment contact form. There is no path at all
> for the problem-aware-but-not-ready visitor, who is the overwhelming majority of
> anyone arriving from non-branded search. The funnel has a top that is closed.

## 8. Handley, *Everybody Writes* / Maslen, *Seductive Web Copy* / *Content Chemistry*

Concrete, specific, reader-centred language; "utility × inspiration × empathy";
write to one person. Maslen: features describe, benefits sell, and specificity is
what makes a benefit believable.

> **Applied:** the Prism page uses the word "structured" nine times. [FACT] Every
> instance is a word doing the job a specific noun should do. "Structured
> assessment" tells the buyer nothing; "we interview your five most senior people
> and map every handoff between them" tells them everything.

## The synthesis the books converge on

Five independent authors, from different traditions, point at the same defect:

| Book | Diagnosis of this site |
|---|---|
| Sheridan | You hide the five things buyers actually want to know |
| Hormozi | Your real advantage (speed, fixed scope) is buried in a policy note |
| Miller | You are the hero of your own website; it fails the grunt test |
| Blanks & Jesson | You cannot test your way out of this; rewrite, don't tweak |
| Krug / Ash | You force the visitor to self-diagnose, then offer one high-commitment door |

> [INFER] The books do not recommend more traffic. They recommend that this
> company become **specific, priced, named and answerable** first — because traffic
> sent to the current site would convert at close to nothing, and because the
> content that earns the traffic is the same content that builds the trust. That
> ordering is the strategy. See `06`.
