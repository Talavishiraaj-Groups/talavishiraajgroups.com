# 08 — Directories and listing platforms

`05` established that directories matter here for one evidenced reason: **on the SERP
for `ai automation agency for small businesses`, DesignRush and G2 rank on page one.**
[FACT] The directories *are* the search result. Ranking in them is a way into SERPs the
domain cannot reach directly.

This file names them, with verified costs and — more importantly — the **gates**, which
turn out to be the whole story.

Everything below is from the platforms' own pages unless marked otherwise.

---

## The finding that governs this entire file

**Both of the two credible directories require client references before you can rank.**

- **Clutch:** Premier Verified requires **3+ reviews, one within the past 12 months,
  3.0+ average rating**. [FACT] Ranking is driven by review volume and recency.
- **GoodFirms:** research verification includes *"2–3 client reference calls"*, and
  *"about 23% of applicants are accepted"*, in 5–10 business days. [FACT]

`06` and `07` independently identified "get three clients on record with a number" as
the blocking dependency for Big-5 topic 4 (reviews) and for Norman's reflective layer.

> [INFER] It is the same three conversations. One action — asking three existing clients
> to go on record — simultaneously unblocks: the reviews content, the site's entire
> social-proof layer, Clutch ranking, and GoodFirms acceptance. **Nothing else in this
> research project has that leverage.** It should be done before any listing is created,
> because a profile with zero reviews on a review-ranked platform is inert.

## Tier 1 — Do these. Free, credible, evidenced.

### 1. Clutch — `clutch.co/get-listed`
- Basic profile **free**, no card. Manually reviewed by an analyst, **~3 days** to
  publish. Accepts "most business service providers with functional websites and
  verifiable full-time employees." [FACT]
- Free tier includes: directory inclusion by service line, review collection, basic
  analytics, portfolio, up to **3 shareable case studies**, and **automatic inclusion on
  The Manifest** (Clutch's sister site — a second listing for one action). [FACT]
- **Important limitation of the free tier: no "Visit Website" button and no direct link
  from directory pages.** Those are Verified-only. [FACT]
  > [INFER] So the free Clutch listing is a *visibility and trust* asset, **not** a
  > backlink. Anyone who tells you to list on Clutch "for the link" is wrong on the free
  > tier. Value it for the SERP presence and the buyer trust instead.
- Clutch's own note: *"In most cases, buyers find the company they want to work with by
  landing on Reviews Pages where your company is listed, not by using the search
  feature."* [FACT] → the category page is the asset, so service-line selection matters
  more than profile prose.
- Paid: **Clutch Verified $499/year** ($42/mo billed annually) — adds the website link,
  verified badge, priority visibility, unlimited case studies, phone-interview reviews,
  competitor-free profile. Verification uses business registration + a **CreditSafe
  credit check**. [FACT]
- Sponsorship is a separate, far more expensive product: reported **$1,500–$15,000+/month**,
  12-month lock-in. [OBS — third-party reporting, not Clutch's published rate]

**Verdict: claim the free profile. Do not pay for anything yet.** Revisit the $499
Verified tier once three reviews exist, because the website link plus badge then has
something to stand on. `[HYP]` A new company with no reviews buying Verified is paying
for placement above nothing.

> Note for this specific company: Clutch verification runs on business registration and
> credit data, via Secretary of State "or international equivalents." A 2025-founded
> Indian private company with a UK-resident founder may verify slowly or awkwardly.
> [HYP] Expect friction; the reviews route to Verified status is the fallback Clutch
> itself documents.

### 2. GoodFirms — `goodfirms.co`
- **Free forever**, and unusually, the free tier is *fully functional*: complete profile,
  portfolio, **verified buyer reviews and star ratings**, review-collection tools, and
  **algorithmic Leader Matrix ranking**. [FACT]
- GoodFirms states explicitly: *"Star ratings and Leader Matrix positions are never for
  sale."* [FACT]
- Gate: 4-step research verification including **2–3 client reference calls**;
  **~23% acceptance**; 5–10 business days. [FACT]
- Paid PRO: **$49/mo / $588/yr** — featured placement, unlimited RFP applications,
  analytics, premium RFP portal. Sponsorship is separate and auction-based. [FACT]
  (Third-party reports quote higher "$200–$1,500/month" premium figures — [OBS], treat
  the platform's own $588/yr as the reliable number.)

**Verdict: the highest-value free listing available, and the strictest gate.** Genuinely
useful for an India-HQ company — GoodFirms has strong South Asia coverage. [OBS] But do
not apply until the three client references are lined up, or you burn a 23%-acceptance
application.

### 3. DesignRush — `designrush.com`
- Free basic listing, **genuinely free, no card, no time limit.** Appears in category and
  location directories. [FACT]
- **Ranks on page one for `ai automation agency for small businesses`** — verified
  directly in this research. [FACT] That is the reason to be here.
- DesignRush uses an **internal analyst team**, not client reviews, to evaluate agencies.
  [OBS — third-party] So it is the one credible directory that is **not blocked by the
  reviews dependency**, which makes it the first one to action.
- Paid tiers, published: Marketplace Membership **$200 / $300 / $500 per month, 1-year
  minimum secured upfront**; Sponsorship from **~$2,000/month** for Standard. [FACT]
- Independent assessment is lukewarm on paid ROI: *"Free listings are worth having. Paid
  sponsorships are a harder case to make."* Pricing/value scored 5.5/10. [OBS]

**Verdict: claim the free listing first, this week. Ignore every paid tier.** No
annual commitment without conversion evidence.

### 4. Tool-vendor partner and expert directories — the highest-intent option
Someone searching a vendor's own expert directory has already chosen the tool and has
budget. Intent is far higher than a general agency directory.

- **n8n Expert Partners** — verified: **closed pilot**, requires automation/AI as *main
  revenue stream* and **≥3 customers actively using n8n**; regions UK & Ireland, Northern
  Europe, North America. [FACT] Talavishiraaj's UK presence fits the region; the rest
  does not yet. Community is ~200,000 members and free to join now. [FACT]
- **Make, Zapier, HubSpot, Airtable, Monday** partner programmes — all exist; each has
  its own certification and customer-count gates. Not individually verified this
  session. [HYP] Worth a session of its own.
- **agentlist.directory** — niche AI-agent agency directory, **1,871 agencies listed**,
  filters by framework (LangChain, CrewAI, AutoGen, n8n) and use case. [FACT] Small, but
  precisely targeted and free to try.

**Verdict: pursue as a deliberate sequence, not a one-off.** These are earned, not
bought, and the qualification requirements are themselves a useful forcing function.

### 5. Wellfound — already listed
The company **already has a Wellfound profile** (found during discovery, listing
Bangalore + Dubai/Europe/US/Germany/UK). [FACT] It is a talent platform, not a buyer
channel, but it is an existing indexed citation. **Free. Just make it accurate and
consistent with the site.**

# Part B — Fast inbound: Product Hunt, launch platforms and the 400-directory ecosystem

Added after a direct question about Product Hunt and faster inbound. Researched properly,
and the answer splits sharply in two.

## The unlock that governs all of Part B

**Almost every platform in Part B requires a *product*, not a service.** Product Hunt,
BetaList, Uneed, MicroLaunch, SaaSHub, AlternativeTo, There's An AI For That, Futurepedia,
G2, Capterra — all list things a visitor can click and use.

Talavishiraaj currently has **nothing that qualifies.** A consulting engagement cannot be
submitted to any of them. Today the entire channel is closed.

> [INFER] The **free diagnostic instrument** (`05` #2, `07 §1`, and now `09` — where two
> direct competitors are verified running exactly this as their front door) is what opens
> it. It is not merely an SEO asset and a commitment ladder. It is **the only thing that
> makes this company eligible for several hundred free listing platforms.**
>
> That reframes its priority. One build unlocks: non-branded SEO, the commitment ladder,
> partner collateral, Reddit-answer payload, AI-assistant citations, and the whole
> launch-platform ecosystem. Nothing else in this research has that breadth.

## Product Hunt — honest verdict: not yet, and not for the firm

### What the evidence says

| Finish | Launch-day visitors | Signups |
|---|---|---|
| #1–3 | 5,000–15,000 | 100–400 |
| #4–10 | 1,000–3,000 | 30–100 |
| #11–30 | 300–700 | 10–30 |
| #31+ | under 300 | under 10 |

[OBS — aggregated third-party launch data, 2026; directionally consistent across four
independent sources]

The numbers that matter more than the table:

- **Featured rate has collapsed to ~10%**, from a majority in 2020–2023. 500+ submissions
  a day. If you are not featured, nothing else matters. [OBS]
- **Base rate of a top-3 finish is below 5%**, and "most of that is reserved for products
  with pre-existing distribution." [OBS]
- **Effort: 50–120 hours over 4–6 weeks.** [OBS]
- **B2B converts at roughly half of B2C.** "Book a demo buttons tank the rate." [OBS]
- About **60% of all launch traffic arrives in the first 24 hours**; below top-3 the page
  is "effectively dead by day 14." [OBS]

### The single most useful data point found

A first-hand retrospective (September 2026) from a team that **won #1 Product of the Day**
with 410 upvotes:

- **539 sessions over two days.** Against a pre-launch baseline of 1,930 sessions/day — so
  the entire effect came to roughly **28% of one ordinary day's traffic.** [FACT — their
  own GA4]
- 479 landed on the homepage; **23 reached a page with a price on it** (4.3%), with a 50%
  launch discount live throughout. [FACT]
- Their conclusion: "much closer to a good newsletter send than to a growth event." And
  the decisive admission: "The single biggest lever was that we already had tens of
  thousands of users before launch day." [FACT]

### Applied to Talavishiraaj

Four independent reasons it fails right now:

1. **No audience.** 143 LinkedIn followers. Top-3 finishes go to products with existing
   distribution; the #1 account above was carried by tens of thousands of existing users.
2. **The buyer is not there.** The research is blunt: "If your customer is a chiropractor,
   a plumber, or a dental-practice manager, those customers are not on Product Hunt... a
   launch for a vertical SaaS gets you 800 visitors who will never buy." [OBS] A
   founder-led $500k–$5M services business is the same case. Product Hunt's audience is
   makers, tech-product founders and investors.
3. **Nothing to launch** (see the unlock above).
4. **50–120 hours** is the founder's delivery capacity — the scarcest resource (`05`).

**Verdict: do not launch on Product Hunt now.** [INFER] Expected value is negative.
Revisit only for the free instrument once it exists and has some audience behind it, and
even then treat it as **a permanent DR-91 dofollow backlink plus a badge**, not a traffic
event. Point the launch link at the instrument, never the homepage — the #1 account above
lost 479 of 539 visitors to exactly that mistake. [OBS]

## What actually produces faster, compounding inbound

The most useful comparison found, and the direct answer to the question:

| | Product Hunt | 10+ product directories |
|---|---|---|
| Day-1 traffic | 300–4,000 (strong) | 2–20 (minimal) |
| Month-3 traffic | near zero | 50–500/mo, **compounding** |
| Backlink value | mostly nofollow, limited | many dofollow, DR 50–91 |
| Setup | 2–4 hrs + 4–6 weeks prep | ~3–5 hrs total |
| Cost | free | free (basic tiers) |
| **Requires existing audience?** | **Yes** | **No** |
| 12-month signups | 50–400, mostly day 1 | 100–600, distributed |

[OBS — single third-party comparison; figures are directional only]

> [INFER] For a company with **no audience**, the "requires existing audience" row decides
> it. Product Hunt is a megaphone that only works if you already have a crowd; directories
> work precisely when you do not. That is the answer to "which drives inbound faster" —
> counter-intuitively the slow-looking channel is the faster one here, because the
> fast-looking one is gated on something the company does not have.

> [INFER] The mechanism is sound regardless of that statistic: LLMs answering "what tool

## A warning about the sources in Part B

Stated plainly, because it affects how much weight to give the above:

- **Most of these lists are published by companies selling submission services** at $99+.
  Their claims — "increase your Domain Rating up to DR 20", "DR mid-20s to DR 40+ in three
  months through directory submissions alone" — are **marketing, not evidence**.
  [UNVERIFIED] Treat every DR-gain promise as unproven.
- **The DR figures disagree across sources.** Clutch is given as both DR 91 and DR 79;
  GoodFirms as 55 and 68; BetaList as 67 and 72; Futurepedia as 42 and 67. [FACT] Nobody
  should plan on a specific number.
- Several entries require a **reciprocal backlink** for the free tier (AITop10.tools is
  explicit). [FACT] That is a link exchange. Decline — a reciprocal-link footprint is a
  liability, consistent with the Tier-3 reasoning in Part A.
- Do not pay a submission service. The value is in ~20 relevant listings, not 400. One list
  says it outright: "Twenty relevant listings beat a hundred random ones." [FACT]
