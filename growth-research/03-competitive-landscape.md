## Part B — Who actually wins the winnable queries

Live SERP evidence, gathered this session.

### Query: `operations consultant for small business fixed price operations audit`

Ranking set included **covisionconsultants.com (twice)**, scaleupexec.com,
velox.consulting, parakeeto.com, asamby.com, aurorafinancials.com, kamyarshah.com.
[FACT]

Not one is a large firm. [OBS]

> [INFER] **This space is winnable by a six-person company.** The incumbents are
> boutiques and solo operators competing on published thinking, not on domain
> authority or budget. This is the single most encouraging finding in the project.

### The pricing reality — and Prism is under-priced

Figures stated openly on those ranking pages: [FACT — as published by them; not
independently audited]

| Engagement | Published range | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Process audit (one workflow) | $2,000 – $8,000 | — |
| Focused operations audit | $5,000 – $15,000 | 2–4 weeks |
| Operations audit (whole operating system) | $5,000 – $25,000 | 3–6 weeks |
| "Diagnostic or audit" | $10,000 – $25,000 | — |
| AI discovery / strategy audit | $5,000 – $15,000 | — |
| Written diagnosis + prioritised plan (15+ employees) | $3,000 – $8,000 | 2 weeks |

Prism, per the internal notes: **$2,499 in 10–13 business days.** [UNVERIFIED price]

> [INFER] Prism sits at or below the floor of every published band, while
> delivering *faster* than all of them. Two readings, and they are not exclusive:
> (i) the price is a genuine wedge, but (ii) at $2,499 against a $5k–$25k norm it
> risks reading as *"this must be shallow"* — a Perceived-Likelihood problem
> (`04 §2`), not a bargain. Buyers of judgement use price as a quality signal.
>
> [HYP] Worth testing: Prism at $4,900–$6,500, positioned on the 10-day turnaround
> against a 3–6 week norm. The speed advantage is real and currently free.

### What the winning page actually does

`covisionconsultants.com/operations-audit-for-small-business/` — fetched and read in
full. [FACT] It is worth studying closely because it is the playbook, executed:

- **Bylined** to a named human (Wissam Bazzi), dated, ~15 min read.
- **Proprietary first-party data as the hook:** *"Across 30+ operations audits at
  Covision, we've found the same pattern... 15 to 25 percent of operational cost is
  pure waste, hiding in plain sight inside handoffs."*
- **Gives the entire methodology away** — all 8 phases, with the frameworks named
  (SIPOC, Value Stream Mapping) and external authorities cited (ASQ, Lean
  Enterprise Institute).
- **Answers the money question in a table**, including competitors' ranges.
- **A comparison table** (operations vs financial vs compliance vs process audit)
  that captures the entire adjacent query space on one page.
- **A "7 warning signs" self-diagnosis list**, with an explicit threshold: *"If
  three or more of those sound familiar, you have a clear business case."*
- **Three embedded interactive tools** — readiness quiz, savings estimator, audit
  scorer — labelled **"2 min · No email required."**
- Site-wide header banner carries a named client result.

> [INFER] This is Sheridan's Big 5 and Schwartz's product-led SEO in one artefact,
> and it is beating everyone with it. Note especially the **ungated** tool: it
> trades the email for trust, then earns the enquiry from people who self-qualified.
> Compare Talavishiraaj's only conversion path: one high-commitment contact form.

### A direct indictment, from the market itself

One ranking page warns buyers: *"Be cautious of vague deliverables, as vague
deliverables ('we will improve your operations') are a red flag."* Another: a strong
proposal *"specifies exactly what will be produced: number of SOPs, specific
frameworks used, KPI dashboard type, implementation support duration."* [FACT]

> The market is **actively teaching buyers to distrust the exact register
> Talavishiraaj's site is written in.** "Structured strategic recommendations for
> systems improvement and workflow optimization" is the red flag, verbatim.

### Query: `ai automation agency for small businesses`

Ranking set: DesignRush (directory), G2, Forbes, a LinkedIn Pulse article, a
listicle on an agency blog (`jadasquad.com/blog/ai-automation-agencies`), plus
agency sites (theaiautomationagency.ai, mqlflow.com, automaly.io). [FACT]

Competitor positioning observed, all of which lead with a **measured outcome and a
clock**: "measurable ROI within 30 days"; "ROI-led delivery in 4–6 weeks";
"practical, tailored... measurable ROI". [FACT — their own claims]

Published AI-automation pricing on those pages: discovery/strategy audits
$5,000–$15,000; single-flow builds $3,000–$15,000. Also the claim that AI-automation
pricing fell ~35% between 2024 and 2026. [FACT that they claim it; the 35% figure is
[UNVERIFIED] and should not be repeated as fact]

> [INFER] Three routes into this SERP that do not require domain authority:
> (1) directory listings (DesignRush, Clutch, G2) — these *are* the SERP;
> (2) being included in other people's listicles — a link-earning and
> citation-earning motion, not a content motion;
> (3) the Big-5 comparison content that Covision proves works.
>
> Note the contrast: every competitor here leads with a number and a deadline.
> Talavishiraaj leads with "structured". Same market, opposite register.

## Part C — The real competitive set, reframed

The notes file organised competitors by what they *are*. More useful is organising
by **which door the buyer walks through**, because that determines the channel.

| Buyer's search | Who they find | Talavishiraaj's real standing |
|---|---|---|
| "my ops are a mess, who audits this" | Covision, ScaleUpExec, Asamby, Velox, solo fractional COOs | **Genuinely competitive** — boutique field, content-decided, and Prism is faster. Best beachhead |
| "automate this with AI" | Automaly, MQLFlow, ResultantAI, XRAY, Luhhu, n8n/Zapier expert directories | **Competitive on capability**, invisible on discovery. Directory listings are the cheap win |
| "build me software / an AI agent" (+ geo) | Large Indian dev shops; "osiz" owns the autocomplete | **Weak.** Commoditised, price-driven, deep incumbents. Avoid as a wedge |
| "GTM / revenue architecture" | GTM Partners, Proofpoint Marketing | **Weak.** Category owned by firms with proprietary research and audiences |
| "fractional COO" | Umbrex network, ELSE/Alex Boch, marketplaces | **Structurally different** — they sell a person, Prism sells an artefact. Not a competitor so much as a **referral partner**: they need diagnosis, Prism supplies it |

> [INFER] The last row is the unexploited one. Solo fractional COOs and boutique ops
> consultants are not rivals to a 10-day fixed-fee diagnostic — they are its natural
> distribution channel. They need a defensible diagnosis to justify a retainer, and
> they cannot produce one profitably themselves. That is a partnership motion with a
> real economic logic on both sides, and it needs no traffic at all.
