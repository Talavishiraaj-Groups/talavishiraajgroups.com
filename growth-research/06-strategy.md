# 06 — Strategy

Synthesis of `01`–`05`. Conclusions only; the evidence lives in those files.

## The core finding

**Talavishiraaj Groups does not have a traffic problem. It has a specificity problem
that makes traffic worthless.**

Three facts, established independently, that together determine everything:

1. The category it named itself into — "workflow intelligence", "operations
   diagnostic" — **has no buyer demand and is already owned by other industries**
   (healthcare IT, VMware). `02` Findings 1–3. [FACT]
2. The demand that does exist is held by **six-person boutiques competing on published
   judgement**, not by firms with budget or authority. `03` Part B. [FACT] The ground
   is winnable.
3. The site has **no non-branded surface area and no proof of any kind** — so traffic
   arriving today would not convert, and no channel can compensate. `01`. [FACT]

> [INFER] Therefore the ordering is forced: **specificity → proof → distribution →
> traffic.** Any plan that starts with traffic wastes the spend. This also happens to
> be the founder's own stated principle — *diagnose before prescription* — which the
> company has applied to its clients and not to itself.

## The central irony to exploit

The company sells the diagnosis of a problem it currently has.

Its own website is: too many things for too many people, no clear owner of the
message, capability fragmented across six buckets, no visible accountability. That is
the founder-led sprawl Prism exists to fix.

> [INFER] This is not an embarrassment, it is the best content angle available.
> *"We ran our own diagnostic on ourselves and here is what it found"* is credible,
> specific, disarming (Sheridan `04 §1`), and nobody else can copy it. It converts the
> company's single biggest weakness into its most distinctive published asset.

## 1. Positioning

**Drop from six capabilities to one wedge.** Not permanently — as the door.

The wedge, on the evidence: **a fixed-fee, 10-day operational diagnosis for
founder-led businesses that have outgrown how they run.**

Why this wedge and not the others (`03` Part C):
- It is the only market where the competitive field is boutiques and the decision is
  made on published thinking. [FACT]
- It is the only one where the company's real advantage — speed and fixed scope — is
  structurally superior to the norm (10–13 days vs a published 2–6 weeks). [FACT]
- "AI agent / software development + geo" is commoditised and deeply held. Avoid.
- "GTM / revenue architecture" is owned by firms with proprietary research. Avoid.

**Vocabulary, decided by evidence not taste:** stop saying *workflow intelligence*.
Say what buyers search — *operations audit*, *operations diagnostic for small
business*, *AI readiness assessment*. `02` Finding 4. The internal name Prism can stay
as the product name; the *category* label must be borrowed from the market (Jakob's
Law, `04 §6`).

**Reconcile the two Prisms.** `01` found a sharp, priced, targeted product in the
internal documents and a vague, unpriced, everyone-shaped engagement on the site. Ship
the internal one. In particular restore the two things the site deleted: **the price**
and **"the single core structural constraint."** One constraint named is a dream
outcome; "systems visibility across the organization" is not.

**Price.** [HYP, needs testing] $2,499 sits below the floor of every published
comparable ($3k–$25k, `03`). Against a market that reads price as a quality signal,
being cheapest while claiming senior judgement is a Perceived-Likelihood liability, not
a wedge. Test $4,900–$6,500, justified on the 10-day turnaround. Publish the number
either way — Sheridan's Big 5 topic 1.

## 2. Search intent

Three tiers, and the site currently serves only the third.

| Buyer state | What they type | What must exist | Exists today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem-aware, not solution-aware | "revenue up but margins flat", "I'm the bottleneck in my business" | Symptom-led diagnostic content + the free instrument | **Nothing** |
| Solution-aware, comparing | "operations audit cost", "operations audit vs process audit", "is an ops audit worth it" | Big-5 content: price, comparisons, problems, who else is good | **Nothing** |
| Vendor-aware | "talavishiraaj groups" | The current 26 pages | All 26 |

> [INFER] The funnel is inverted — 100% of the site serves the smallest and last stage.
> Every page exists for someone who already knows the company. That is the whole
> discoverability problem in one line.

## 3. Content

The backlog is not a topic brainstorm. It is Sheridan's Big 5, applied literally, and
it is entirely unbuilt (`04 §1`):

1. **Cost** — what an operations diagnosis costs, including competitors' ranges and
   what changes the number. The page nobody in this niche writes honestly.
2. **Problems** — when Prism is the *wrong* purchase; what it cannot tell you; what
   goes wrong in diagnostics. Sheridan's disarmament. This is the highest-trust,
   lowest-competition content available.
3. **Comparisons** — diagnostic vs fractional COO vs full consultancy vs doing it
   yourself. Captures the entire adjacent query space on one page, as Covision proves.
4. **Reviews** — currently impossible: zero named clients. **Blocked, and it is the
   most urgent unblock in the project.** Getting three clients to go on record, named,
   with one number each, is worth more than any page on this list.
5. **Best in class** — who else does this well, and when to hire them instead.

Plus the two assets that are not articles:
- **The free diagnostic instrument** — Prism's method, self-serve, ungated (`05` #2).
- **"We diagnosed ourselves"** — the central-irony piece above.

**Register, non-negotiable:** named byline, first person, specific nouns, real numbers,
no "structured" (used 9× on one page today, `01`). The market is *explicitly* warning
buyers against the current register — *"vague deliverables are a red flag"* (`03`).

## 4. Distribution, authority, conversion — how they reinforce each other

The reinforcement is the point, and it runs through one artefact:

```
            label     link to   (05 T3)
   │           │        │            │              │
   └───────────┴────────┴────────────┴──────────────┘
                   │
      self-qualified enquiry, method already trusted
```

- **Positioning** decides the vocabulary → which decides what can be found at all.
- **Content** is the proof, because there are no case studies to be proof instead.
- **The instrument** is simultaneously the SEO asset, the link magnet, the partner
  collateral, the community-answer payload, and the top of the funnel the site
  currently lacks.
- **Authority** comes from a named human, not the company. 143 company followers vs a
  founder with a real thesis: the asset is the person (`05` #3).
- **Conversion** must add a low-commitment door. Today there is exactly one, and it is
  "Initiate Engagement" (Ash, `04 §7`). Self-diagnosis first, conversation second.
- **Sales** closes on the artefact the buyer already used. That is what "discovery to
  qualified commercial conversation" actually means here.

**And run the partnership motion in parallel, because it needs none of the above.**
Fractional COOs need a defensible diagnosis to scope a retainer and cannot produce one
profitably (`05` #5). Thirty accurate conversations, no traffic required. On the
evidence this is the fastest route to revenue, and it buys the patience the content
strategy needs.

## 5. Measurement — deliberately not conversion rate

A/B testing is arithmetically impossible at this traffic (`04 §5`). So the honest
leading indicators are:

- Number of pages that can be found by someone who has never heard the company: **0
  today.**
- Named clients on record with a number: **0 today.**
- Instrument completions.
- Partner conversations held.
- Founder-authored pieces published under his own name.

Not: rankings, sessions, or any conversion-rate target. Those come later and cannot be
read reliably yet.

   "Prism" appears **0 times** on the home page; "systems" appears 28 times, always as a

5. Leave the six capability pages and the governance pages alone. They are genuinely
   good for later-stage buyers and for enterprise reassurance. They are simply not
   discovery assets, and they should stop being asked to do that job.
