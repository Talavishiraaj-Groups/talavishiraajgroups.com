# 09 — The landing page: is it too long?

## The short answer: no. It is too **wide**.

Your instinct that something is wrong is right. The diagnosis is not length. Measured
this session, against the competitors who actually rank and convert:

| Site | Body words | h2 | h3 | H1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ScaleUpExec | 3,281 | 19 | **0** | Fractional COO services for founders who are done being the bottleneck |
| Covision | 2,559 | 10 | 21 | Business Operations Consulting for Companies in Michigan & Across the U.S. |

| **Talavishiraaj** | **1,190** | **11** | **26** | **Solving complex problems with research and engineering** |
| Proofpoint Marketing | 588 | 6 | 13 | Positioning your firm as the lowest-risk choice for your ideal clients |
| Luhhu | 440 | 3 | 8 | Zapier Automation, Done For You |

[FACT — all measured directly from live HTML]

**Talavishiraaj's home page is the third-shortest in the set and has the most h3s of
any of them.** Median competitor length is ~1,350 words; the two longest are 2–2.8×
longer. So "too lengthy" is not supported by the evidence — cutting words would not fix
it, and would probably make it worse.

> [INFER] What is actually wrong: **1,190 words carrying 26 parallel sub-items.** The
> page is dense with *choices* rather than long with *argument*. Compare ScaleUpExec:
> 3,281 words, **zero h3s** — nearly three times the length, read as one linear
> narrative. Hick's Law (`07 §2`) says decision time rises with the number and
> complexity of choices; branching feels longer than reading. That is why the page
> *feels* long while measuring short.

The 26 h3s break down as **three parallel lists of six** — six problems, six
capabilities, six audiences — plus five differentiators and three case studies. The
visitor is asked to locate themselves on three separate six-way grids before anything
is offered.

## What every competitor's hero does that this one does not

Verbatim from live pages. [FACT]

**ScaleUpExec:** *"Fractional COO services for founders who are done being the
bottleneck"* → *"You did not start this company to spend every day firefighting
operations."* Hero strip: **"0% client churn · 25+ businesses scaled · results typically
begin in week one · engagements from $5,000/month"**

**ELSE:** *"A company that runs without you. And keeps growing"* → *"I read the numbers,
build the management system and put AI agents inside your processes... I answer for the
result."* Offer: **"25 minutes on video · free · PDF with your bottlenecks after the
call"**

**Luhhu:** *"Zapier Automation, Done For You"* → *"Tell me what you need, I'll quote you,
and get started within hours."* Strip: **"Zapier Gold Solution Partner · 100+ clients
helped · Same-day quotes · No calls required"**

**Proofpoint:** *"Positioning your firm as the lowest-risk choice for your ideal
clients"* → then *"Is your firm a risky bet?"* → CTA: **"💡 Take the Positioning Risk
Assessment"**

**Asamby:** *"Unlock growth with a Fractional COO"* → *"Trusted by founders across the
US, Europe & Middle East"* + client logo carousel.

**Talavishiraaj:** *"Solving complex problems with research and engineering"* → *"We
bring together software engineering, AI, and product development to build practical
solutions."* No proof strip. No number. No price. No named buyer.

### Three patterns, all unanimous across the set

1. **Every H1 names a buyer, an outcome, or a concrete service.** Four of nine name the
   buyer explicitly ("founders who are done being the bottleneck", "for Growing
   Businesses", "for your ideal clients"). Talavishiraaj's names a *method*
   ("research and engineering") — the only one that does. [OBS]
2. **Proof and price sit in the hero, not deep in the page.** Churn rates, client counts,
   partner badges, geographies, and actual monthly fees — above the fold. [FACT]
   Talavishiraaj has none of these anywhere on the site.
3. **Two of the nine lead with an assessment instrument** — Proofpoint's "Positioning
   Risk Assessment" as its primary hero CTA, and ELSE's free *"PDF with your bottlenecks
   after the call."*

> [INFER] Point 3 matters more than the rest. ELSE's entire funnel is *free
> mini-diagnosis → paid engagement* — which is Prism's own model, given away as the top
> of the funnel. The instrument recommended in `05`/`07` from psychology and SERP
> evidence is **already running as the front door of two direct competitors.** It is not
> a theory; it is the observed market standard for selling a diagnostic.

## The structural problem: the product is not on the page

Already in `07` Part 6, restated here because it is a landing-page decision:

The visitor scrolls eight sections and **never encounters Prism.** "Prism" appears **0
times** on the home page. [FACT] The page offers six open-ended capabilities and one
high-commitment form — and omits the single scoped, priced, buyable thing the company
sells.

Weinschenk's Order Effect (*"First Is Best"*, `07 §1`) says first position carries
disproportionate weight. The thing that should be first is absent entirely.

## Recommended structure — for approval, not yet built

Not shorter. **Narrower, and linear.** Target ~1,200–1,600 words, but with the 26
sub-items cut to roughly 10 and the three six-way grids collapsed.

| # | Section | Why, and which principle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero: named buyer + outcome + the 10-day clock.** One primary CTA (the instrument), one secondary ("Talk to us") | Grunt test (`04 §3`); every competitor H1 does this |
| 2 | **Proof strip** — named clients, one number each, geographies | Social validation (`07 §1`); unanimous in the competitor set. **Blocked on getting 3 clients on record** (`08`) |
| 3 | **"Does this sound like you?"** — 4–5 symptoms in the buyer's own words, with a threshold ("if three or more…") | Covision's verified pattern (`03`); self-identification replaces the six-way sort |
| 4 | **Prism, named and priced.** What you get, the 10–13 days, the fee | Hormozi's bottom of the equation (`04 §2`); Sheridan Big 5 #1 |
| 5 | **The free instrument**, ungated | Reciprocity → commitment ladder (`07 §1`); Proofpoint and ELSE both do this |
| 6 | **One case study with a number**, linked | Norman's reflective level (`07 §4`) |
| 7 | **The founder, named, with a face** and the three principles | Similarity + authority (`07 §1`); he is the strongest asset and is absent |
| 8 | **Capabilities, demoted to one line each** with links out | Hick + Miller; breadth is real, but it is not the front door |
| 9 | **Close: smallest possible next step** | Peak-End (`07 §2`) — not a refund policy |

**What moves off the page:** the six-problem grid, the six-audience grid, two of three
case-study cards, and three of five differentiators. These become the `/services` and
`/case-studies` pages' job — where a visitor who has already self-identified will
actually read them.

> [INFER] Net effect: roughly the same word count, a third of the choices, and the
> product visible in the first screen. The brief was "too lengthy"; the fix is "too many
> doors."
