## 1. What Prism actually is (and what the website says it is)

**Canon:** Prism is a *productized senior-judgment decision engagement*. Its beachhead is
**the build / buy / AI go-no-go**: *"Before you build it, buy it, or bet on AI — Prism is
the independent two-week go/no-go that tells you whether to, and what first. Not your dev
shop. Not a gut-check. Not a $50K deck."* [CANON]

**The live website** calls it a *"Workflow Intelligence System"* and a *"structured
workflow intelligence assessment"*, lists six workflow/systems problems, and never
mentions build/buy or AI investment. [FACT]

> The site is not describing a watered-down version of Prism. **It is describing a
> different product.** Your own instinct — "who would read a product, a workflow
> intelligence system, which is not" — is correct and the canon confirms it.

Canon's explicit **words to avoid**: *diagnostic · audit · assessment · constraint ·
diagnose · recommendations · optimize/fix operations · transform · unlock · holistic ·
end-to-end*. [CANON §11]

The live `/systems` page uses *assessment*, *diagnostic*, *recommendations* and
*optimization* throughout, and the word *structured* nine times. [FACT] **Every one of
those is on the banned list.**

## 2. ⚠️ Three corrections to my earlier research

I was working without the product docs. These are wrong in files `02`, `05`, `06`, `08`:

### Correction 1 — I recommended the banned vocabulary
I recommended repositioning into *"operations audit"* and *"AI readiness assessment"*
because those have search demand (`02` Finding 4). **Canon forbids audit/assessment/
diagnostic framing**, and for a sound reason: it collapses Prism into the diagnostic
cluster whose whole weakness is that it stops before the judgment.

**Resolution — and it is not a compromise.** The canon's *own* beachhead language turns
out to be the better SEO target. See §3. Keep canon's decision vocabulary in the body
copy and brand voice; take the *keyword* from "build vs buy", which is a decision phrase,
not an audit phrase. The two are compatible because they are the same idea in the buyer's
words.

### Correction 2 — the canon says do not build an SEO machine yet
Canon §12 is explicit: **"Unnecessary (do not build yet): a content calendar, an SEO
machine, nurture automation, a full site, a CRM build. Optimize for qualified buyer
intent, not traffic."** With the evidence `[DME-Prism: "42M views, $0" — attention ≠
demand]`. [CANON]

This directly conflicts with the brief to make "SEO, SEM, inbound traffic and domain
authority live when the code goes live."

**Resolution:** separate the two things the canon is separating.
- **A content/SEO *machine* — a calendar, volume publishing, link campaigns, paid search
  (SEM): deferred.** Canon is right. There is no proven demand yet; spending on volume
  before a single paid Prism engagement is the documented failure mode.
- **Technical SEO foundations and *one* correctly-built product page: do now.** These are
  not a machine. They are a one-off, permanent, cheap correctness layer — title, meta,
  canonical, schema, internal links, a URL that matches how buyers search. Shipping a
  page with the wrong URL and wrong name is a cost you pay for years.
- **Canon §12 itself prescribes exactly what to build:** *"one decision-guide per target
  decision + one application path + the qualifying conversation."* That is the product
  page plus the guide plus booking. **That is this build.** Nothing beyond it.

> [INFER] So the honest answer: build the page and the technical foundation properly, and
> do *not* turn on SEM or a content programme. Those wait for a paid signal. I would be
> working against your own canon if I did otherwise, and against the evidence.

## 5. Booking a call — the open-source question, answered factually

You asked specifically: do *we* get an email, do *they* get one, and are there reminders,
like Calendly.

### Cal.com, self-hosted — what the docs actually say

**Confirmations: yes, both sides.** [FACT] Official docs: *"Both you and the booker
receive: Email confirmation, Calendar invitation (.ics file), Meeting location details"* —
implemented as separate templates, `booking-confirmation` (to the booker) and
`organizer-booking-notification` (to you), plus an ICS attachment.

**It requires SMTP configuration.** [FACT] `EMAIL_SERVER_HOST / PORT / USER / PASSWORD /
EMAIL_FROM`, or SendGrid/Resend. Docs are blunt: *"Without email configuration, booking
confirmations won't be sent. This is required for production use."*
> Relevant: this repo already contains `SMTP_SETUP.md` and `ZOHO_SETUP.md`, so mail
> sending is already solved here. [FACT]

**Reminders: yes on self-hosted Cal.com — and this is the detail that decides which
version you use.** [FACT] The Workflows plan-limitations table reads:
*"Free Plan: Can only use the default reminder email (no customization). Team,
Organization, Enterprise: Full custom workflows. **Self-hosted Instances: No restrictions
on workflow usage.**"*
Triggers include *before the event starts*, *when a new event is booked*, *after the event
ends*, *when cancelled/rescheduled*; actions include email to host, email to attendee, SMS,
WhatsApp.

### ⚠️ The trap: there are now two projects, and they are not the same

Cal.com moved its production codebase private. The open-source repo is now **`cal.diy`**.
[FACT]

| | Self-hosted **cal.com** (AGPLv3) | **cal.diy** (MIT) |
|---|---|---|
| Licence | AGPLv3 — network copyleft; commercial licence available | MIT, fully permissive |
| Self-host commercially | ✅ per their own licence table | ✅ |
| **Workflows / reminders** | ✅ no restrictions self-hosted | ❌ **removed** |
| Teams, SSO, Insights | EE-only | ❌ removed |
| Project's own guidance | production-capable | *"strictly recommended for personal, **non-production** use"* |

[FACT — both repos' READMEs and the AGPL licence table]

> [INFER] So: **cal.diy is the wrong choice for this.** It is the more permissive licence,
> but reminders are exactly the feature that was stripped, and the project itself warns
> against production use. If you self-host, self-host the AGPLv3 Cal.com, and be aware
> AGPLv3 is network copyleft — it obliges you to offer source to users who interact with it
> over a network. For a booking widget on a commercial site, read that properly before
> committing.

### Recommendation

**Use Cal.com's free hosted tier, with the embed.** [INFER] Reasoning:
- Confirmations to both parties and the **default reminder email** are included free.
- Zero server administration, zero AGPL question, zero deliverability work.
- The embed drops into the product page; the booking link can live on your own domain
  later if it matters.
- Custom *multi-step* reminder workflows are the paid/self-hosted feature — and canon
  only needs one qualifying call, so the default reminder is sufficient.
- Self-hosting costs founder hours, which canon and `05` both identify as the scarcest
  resource. Revisit only if data residency or branding demands it.

**Alternatives if hosted is unacceptable:** Easy!Appointments (GPLv3, PHP/MySQL, simple,
self-host) and Rallly (self-hostable, but it is group *poll* scheduling, not 1:1 booking —
wrong shape). Neither was verified in depth this session. [HYP]

### One canon constraint on the booking button

Canon §12/§13 is specific: the CTA is **"a hand-raise about *this decision*, not a
newsletter signup"**, and the application **is** the qualifying filter (the four
Prism-worthy conditions). The call is *"one structured working-session call, not a
pitch"*. [CANON]

> [INFER] So the right pattern is **not** a bare "Book a call" button. It is a short
> qualifying form — what decision are you facing, what does it commit, is it settled — and
> booking offered *on qualification*. An unqualified calendar link invites exactly the
> conversations canon says to decline, and spends the founder's hours. Label it for the
> decision ("Bring us your decision" / "Get the go/no-go"), not for the meeting.

## 6. The hero line

Your line, and it is canon-consistent: *"Before you buy it, build it, or AI decision —
Know what matters the most."*

Canon §4 item 5 is literally *"Determining what matters most — separating the decisive
factors from the noise."* [CANON] And the positioning statement opens *"Before you build
it, buy it, or bet on AI."* [CANON] So the sentiment is right and sourced.

Grammatically it needs a small fix. Options that keep your words and your meaning:

1. **"Before you build it, buy it, or bet on AI — know what matters most."** ← closest to
   canon's own phrasing; recommended
2. "Before you build it, buy it, or bet on AI. Know what matters most."
3. "Build it, buy it, or bet on AI? Know what matters most — before you commit."

> [INFER] Recommend #1, with the product name **Prism** above it and the mechanism
> immediately below: *the independent two-week go/no-go.* "Know what matters most" is
> the promise; it is not specific enough to stand alone, so the sentence under it must
> carry the what-you-get.
