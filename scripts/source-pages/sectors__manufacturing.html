Questions we are asked

What does manufacturing technology work involve?
Line and quality data that exists but never reaches the people deciding what to run next, or arrives a shift too late. The work is usually less about building something new and more about making what already exists agree with itself: integrating the systems holding the data, establishing which numbers can be trusted, and putting the result where a decision gets made. We cover software and systems engineering, applied AI and automation, and the hardware side where the problem reaches it.
How do you know whether this is worth building?
We look at volume, repeatability and what an error costs before proposing anything, and we say so when the work does not justify itself. Published data puts projects that defined the requirement before committing within about 10 to 15 percent of budget, against 30 to 60 percent over for projects that skipped it. The assessment is deliberately structured so that it can end in a no.
Have you worked in manufacturing before?
Yes. Our published case studies are anonymized at our clients' request, so the engagements in this sector are not listed by name, and we will not imply otherwise. What we can do on a call is talk through the specific problem shape, what the work involved and what it took to make it hold up in production.

All of this assumes the decision to build is already made. If it is not, and the commitment is large, Prism settles that first.
