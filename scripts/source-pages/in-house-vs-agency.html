Choosing a route

In house, agency, or an independent read first

Build in house when the capability is core to your business and you genuinely have the people free. Hire an agency when the requirement is settled and what you lack is capacity. Bring in a consultancy when the question is organizational rather than technical. Get an independent read first when you cannot yet say, with evidence, that the commitment is the right one.

The table below sets out what each route costs you, who owns the outcome, and what happens when the answer turns out to be wrong. The thing most comparisons skip is whether the decision is settled yet: if it is, this is a capacity question, and if it is not, you are about to buy a build to answer something a build cannot answer.

We do one of these four. The table says where the other three are better, because a comparison where one column wins every row is marketing, and you would be right to discard it.

The row that decides it

Every route above except one is paid according to whether the work happens. Your own team wants the project, an agency is paid to build it, and a consultancy is paid for the phase after the recommendation. None of that makes any of them dishonest. It does mean that if you ask them whether to proceed, you are asking a question of someone whose answer costs them something.

That is the entire argument for doing a small, separate piece of work first, and it is the only row in the table where the fourth column wins outright. If your decision is already settled, it is not worth doing, and the other three columns are where you should be looking.

If the decision is not settled and that is the position you are in, this is what that looks like as a piece of work.

Questions people ask when choosing

Should I build software in-house or hire an agency?
Build in-house when the capability is core to your business, the requirement is well understood, and you genuinely have the people free. Hire an agency when the requirement is settled and what you lack is capacity. The honest warning on in-house is that the cost is hidden rather than absent: it is the roadmap those people stop working on. The honest warning on an agency is that a build quote typically covers the build and not the integration or the years of maintenance after it.
What is the difference between a development agency and a consultancy?
An agency builds what you ask for and is paid to build it. A consultancy analyses the problem and hands over a recommendation, usually without building anything. The practical consequence is that an agency has no commercial reason to tell you the project should not happen, and a consultancy has no obligation to make its recommendation work.
Why would I pay for an assessment instead of just starting the build?
Because the assessment is the cheapest of the four routes and the only one that can end with you spending nothing further. Published data shows projects that defined the requirement before committing land within roughly 10 to 15 percent of budget, while projects that skipped that step run 30 to 60 percent over. If the answer is that the build is right, you have lost very little. If it is not, you have avoided the whole number.
Is an independent assessment just a sales process for the build?
It is a fair question to ask of anyone who offers both. The test is whether the engagement is structured to end. Ask what the deliverable is if the answer is no, whether the fee changes depending on the recommendation, and whether you can take the output to a different firm. If the answers are vague, treat the assessment as a proposal.

Whichever route you take, the published market evidence on budget is in what this work actually costs.
