Logistics software development cost in 2026 runs from about $35,000 for a tool that handles one workflow to more than $550,000 for a multi-site enterprise program, on planning ranges [JustSoftware published](https://justsoftware.io/blogs/logistics-software-development-cost) on 6 August. The bigger question for most carriers, distributors and shippers is whether to build at all, because a lot of logistics work is already sold by the month. ## How much does custom logistics software development cost?

Scope sets the band and the number of systems it has to talk to sets where you land inside it. A dispatch screen for one depot and a platform that links a warehouse, a fleet and three carriers are different purchases.

Most published figures come from development agencies, so we used three that show their working. JustSoftware prices by scope tier. LOW/CODE Agency prices by build approach and app type. Omega ran its own census of ranking pages in September 2026 and found that six of seven started at exactly $30,000, which tells you how much of this content is copied.

| What you are building | Typical cost (2026) | Typical timeline | Biggest cost driver |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One workflow tool, low-code (dispatch board, yard log, reporting) | $35,000 to $80,000 | 4 to 12 weeks | Each major integration, $8,000 to $20,000 |
| Focused custom product, one workflow | $35,000 to $75,000 | Not published | Number of partner connections |
| Connected platform: several modules, partner links, analytics | $100,000 to $220,000 | Not published | Carrier and customer integrations (EDI and APIs) |
| Traditional custom code, mid to large app | $150,000 to $500,000 | 4 to 12 months | Team location and seniority |
| Enterprise program: multi-site, optimization, IoT, migration | $250,000 to $550,000+ | Often 18 months or more | Data migration and live cutover |

The first and fourth rows are LOW/CODE Agency's figures, the second, third and fifth are JustSoftware's. The 18 month figure is the view of a supply chain software vendor in a sponsored FreightWaves piece (more below). The driver column is our reading of where the hours go. Treat all of it as planning ranges: JustSoftware itself says its tiers are not fixed quotes or measured market averages.

### Why the same build can cost $24,000 or $240,000

Team rates swing the total more than features do. Omega shows this with one real project: a courier marketplace built by four people (front end, back end, DevOps and a project manager) in six weeks, which comes to 960 hours.

It then priced those hours at rates other firms publish. The result runs from $24,000 to $48,000 for a South Asian team at $25 to $50 an hour, to $96,000 to $240,000 for a US or Canadian team at $100 to $250 an hour.

| Team location | Hourly rate | Same 960 hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South Asia | $25 to $50 | $24,000 to $48,000 |
| Eastern Europe | $40 to $80 | $38,400 to $76,800 |
| Western Europe | $80 to $180 | $76,800 to $172,800 |
| US and Canada | $100 to $250 | $96,000 to $240,000 |

Rates are the ones Omega collected from App Verticals and Saigon Technology. A cheaper rate is fine when the scope is written down and someone on your side reviews the work every week. It gets expensive when the spec is loose, because the hours grow and the time zone slows every answer.

Hiring is a third option. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the [median software developer wage](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) at $135,980 a year in May 2025, before benefits and recruiting. ## Build or subscribe? A three year worked example

For standard delivery work, a subscription is usually cheaper for the first three years. Building wins when the workflow itself is your edge or no product fits how you operate.

Take a regional distributor running about 4,000 local deliveries a month that wants dispatch, route planning, driver apps and proof of delivery. [Onfleet's pricing page](https://onfleet.com/pricing) lists its Scale plan from $1,349 a month for 5,000 tasks, with route optimization and an API. At that list price, three years is about $48,600, before text message charges, which Onfleet bills separately.

Against that, we priced two custom options from JustSoftware's tiers: the build in year one, then two years of upkeep at 15 to 25 percent of the build.

Only the cheapest custom case comes close to the subscription, and it would cover one workflow rather than everything Onfleet ships. That is why we tell most operators to subscribe for commodity jobs (dispatch, telematics, proof of delivery) and spend their build budget on the part no product does well for them. That might be a pricing engine, a customer portal tied to your own contracts, or the integration layer that joins the systems you already pay for.

The sum flips at higher volume. Subscription prices climb with tasks, vehicles or drivers, while a custom tool's running cost mostly does not. ## What moves the number most?

Four things decide whether you land at the bottom or the top of a range.

1. Integrations. JustSoftware notes that one modern API might add weeks, while several partners with different electronic data interchange (EDI) maps can shape a whole release. LOW/CODE Agency prices each major integration at $8,000 to $20,000 on low-code, and a new carrier API or data field later at $5,000 to $15,000.
2. Legacy data and live cutover. Moving years of orders and customer records while trucks keep running is where enterprise budgets go. JustSoftware adds a 15 to 20 percent contingency when legacy integrations or a live migration are involved. Our [ERP integration guide](/insights/erp-integration) covers the systems most logistics tools have to read from.
3. Optimization and tracking. Route optimization, load planning and live device data move a project up a tier. Buy these as components when you can.
4. Running costs. Budget upkeep as a yearly line from day one. On LOW/CODE Agency's figures, a production low-code app needs $5,000 to $15,000 a month of ongoing work, and a traditional mid-complexity app $20,000 to $60,000 a month.

## How long does logistics software take to build?

A single workflow tool takes weeks. A connected platform takes months. A multi-site program can take more than a year, and that is where buyers get hurt.

LOW/CODE Agency puts low-code builds at 6 to 12 weeks and traditional custom code at 4 to 12 months. At the large end, Nate Watson of Vantage 9 told [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/vantage-9-supply-chain-software-go-live) on 19 November that traditional supply chain system projects take 18 to 24 months and often run late. The piece is sponsored and Vantage 9 sells an alternative, but the warning is fair: by month 18, your carriers, customers and volumes have changed.

The fix is a phased release. Ship the one workflow that saves the most hours first, run it at one site, then add modules. Our [supply chain visibility guide](/insights/supply-chain-visibility) shows where that first release usually pays back fastest.

## What changes in the UK, UAE and Europe?

The engineering costs much the same everywhere. What differs is the paperwork your software has to produce.

In the EU, the [electronic freight transport information (eFTI) regulation](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/logistics-and-multimodal-transport/efti-regulation_en) applies in full from 9 July 2027. From then, authorities in every member state must accept freight documents shared electronically from certified eFTI platforms. The Commission plans to adopt the certification rules by December 2026. If you move goods in Europe, ask any vendor whether their platform will be certified, or whether your custom build will connect to one.

For the UK and the UAE we found no rule in the same window that changes the build budget on its own. Treat customs, data protection and hosting location as items to check with your adviser rather than as a fixed cost line.

## Questions to ask a vendor before you sign

Ask for an itemized quote, then ask these. The answers tell you more than the total does.

```steps
["Which tier is this priced as, and what is explicitly out of scope?", "How many hours does the estimate assume, at what rate, and from which country?", "Which carrier, customer, ERP and warehouse integrations are included, and are they EDI, API or file based?", "Is there an existing product that does 80 percent of this, and why are we not using it?", "What is the release plan, and what goes live at one site first?", "What will hosting, support and upkeep cost each year, priced against a named scope?", "Who owns the source code, data, infrastructure accounts and documentation when the work ends?"]
```

Red flags: a single fixed price with no hours behind it, a plan with one big go-live date a year or more away, and an integration line that does not name the systems. If you have not decided whether to build at all, read [build vs buy](/build-vs-buy) first.
